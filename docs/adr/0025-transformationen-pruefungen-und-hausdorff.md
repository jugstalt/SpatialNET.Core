# Transformations, checks and Hausdorff distance

Three small groups of helpers that hosts keep writing themselves. **Transformations** move, scale and rotate a geometry (`Translate`, `Scale`, `Rotate`, and the general `Transform`). **Checks** answer a yes/no question about the form of a geometry (`IsSimple`, `IsClosed`, `IsRing`, `EqualsExact`). **`HausdorffDistance`** measures how far two shapes are from each other as a whole, not at their closest point. None of them runs the overlay core, and none needs a grid.

*German version: [0025-transformationen-pruefungen-und-hausdorff.de.md](0025-transformationen-pruefungen-und-hausdorff.de.md)*

## Consequences

- **Placement:** `Translate`, `Scale`, `Rotate` and `Transform` in the new static class `Transformations`; `IsSimple`, `IsClosed`, `IsRing` and `EqualsExact` in the new static class `Characteristics`; `HausdorffDistance` in `Measures`. All in `SpatialNET.Extensions`, namespace `SpatialNET`. For the package validation this is an addition only.
- **Transformations** (results through the factory, restricted to `SupportsZ` and `SupportsM`, ADR 0019):
  - `Transform(Func<Coordinate, Coordinate>)` maps every vertex. `Translate(dx, dy)`, `Scale(sx, sy, origin?)` and `Rotate(angle, origin?)` are special cases; they change X and Y and **keep Z and M**. Angles are radians, counter-clockwise; the default origin of `Scale` and `Rotate` is (0, 0). Multiples of a quarter turn are exact (no sine and cosine error).
  - There are overloads for `IPoint2D`, `IMultiPoint2D`, `IPolyline2D`, `IPolygon2D` and `IGeometry2D`, as for `Densify`: the typed ones return the same kind.
  - A polygon is normalised first (ADR 0003) and **its orientation is restored afterwards**: a mirroring transformation (a negative scale factor) turns shells clockwise, so the rings are reversed back, and the result is again valid. `Transform` with an arbitrary function can produce an invalid polygon (it is a coordinate map and not a topology-preserving one); `Translate`, `Rotate` and `Scale` with a factor other than 0 keep validity. A factor of 0 collapses the geometry onto a line or a point.
  - **Exceptions (ADR 0012):** `ArgumentNullException` for `null`, `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` for a `NaN` or infinite parameter (shift, factor, angle, origin), `InvalidOperationException` without a factory, and also if the mapping, or an overflow of the arithmetic, produces a coordinate whose X or Y is `NaN` or infinite. Empty geometries are copied.
- **`IsSimple`:** a point and a polygon are simple (validity of an area is `IsValid`, as in JTS). A multipoint is simple if no point occurs twice. A polyline is simple if its segments meet only where the OGC allows: consecutive segments of one path, the two ends of a closed path, and the end points of two different **open** paths (any number of open paths may end in one point). Everything else is a violation: a crossing, a path that touches or revisits itself, an overlap, a path end on the inside of another segment, and any touch of the closing point of a closed path by another path. The decision uses the exact orientation test and a `SpatialIndex` over the segments, so it is not quadratic. NTS is the oracle.
- **`IsClosed(IPolyline2D)`:** every non-empty path is closed (first and last point equal in X and Y); a polyline without a non-empty path is not closed. **`IsRing(IPolyline2D)`:** exactly one non-empty path, closed, at least four points, and simple.
- **`EqualsExact(a, b, tolerance = 0)`:** the same kind and structure with the same coordinates **as given**, vertex by vertex: the order of points, paths and rings, the start and the direction of a ring all count (the closing point of a ring need not be stored). Two vertices are equal if their distance is at most the `tolerance` (default 0 = exact). Z and M are ignored, as in the topological predicates. For equality of the point sets use `EqualsTopo`.
- **`HausdorffDistance(a, b, densifyFraction = 1)`:** the **discrete** Hausdorff distance as JTS defines it: the larger of the two directed distances, each the greatest distance from a sample point of one geometry to the line work of the other. A polygon counts by its rings, not by its area. The sample points are the vertices; a fraction below 1 divides every segment into `round(1 / fraction)` pieces and adds those points. The result is exact for the samples and a **lower bound** of the true Hausdorff distance, which approaches it with a smaller fraction. An exact Hausdorff distance is not offered. `NaN` for an empty geometry; a fraction outside (0, 1], or one so small that the number of samples is unreasonable, throws `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`. The search uses a `SpatialIndex` over the segments of the other geometry.
- **Tests (ADR 0008):** these methods have no topological edge cases the tag catalog is made for, so `[Covers]` tests take the place of fixtures: hand-written tables, and property tests against NTS: `AffineTransformation` for the combination of rotation, scale and translation; `IsSimple`, `IsClosed` and `IsRing` on lines on a small integer grid (many touches), 20,000 cases; `DiscreteHausdorffDistance` for random polygons, lines and multipoints with several densify fractions. Rigid motions keep area and validity, mirroring keeps the orientation.
- **Playground and documentation:** descriptors for `Translate`, `Scale`, `Rotate`, `IsSimple`, `IsClosed`, `IsRing`, `EqualsExact` and `HausdorffDistance` (ADR 0011; `Transform` takes a function and has none), a section in the concept page for the measures and constructions, glossary entries.

## Considered Options

- Transformations as an `IAffineTransformation` object (rejected: a matrix type is more API than `Translate`, `Scale` and `Rotate`; `Transform` takes any function)
- Rotation about the centre of the envelope by default (rejected: the origin (0, 0) is what JTS does and what a reader expects; an explicit origin is one argument)
- Moving the mod-2 boundary rule into `IsSimple` (rejected: it would call a point where four open paths end non-simple, which JTS does not; the closed-ring rule is the one that matters)
- An exact Hausdorff distance (rejected: it needs the Voronoi diagram of the line work; the discrete one with a fraction is what JTS offers and what users need)
- Comparing Z and M in `EqualsExact` (rejected: Z and M never influence a computation, ADR 0015; a caller who wants them compares the coordinates)
