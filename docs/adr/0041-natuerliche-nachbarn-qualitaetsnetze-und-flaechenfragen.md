# Natural neighbour interpolation, quality meshes and questions about whole surfaces

The terrain methods of ADR 0037 and ADR 0039 answer what a triangulation says *in its triangles*: the height at a point, the slope of a triangle, the volume above a plane. Four questions go beyond that and are asked in practice: **a smooth height** instead of the creases of the linear one, **a finer mesh without sharp triangles** as the input of a calculation, **how much earth lies between two surfaces** (before and after an excavation), and **where it is steep, and what can be seen from where**. This ADR adds them to `Triangulation`.

*German version: [0041-natuerliche-nachbarn-qualitaetsnetze-und-flaechenfragen.de.md](0041-natuerliche-nachbarn-qualitaetsnetze-und-flaechenfragen.de.md)*

## Consequences

- **Placement:** members of the class `Triangulation`, in three partial files in `SpatialNET.Extensions` (`Triangulation.Interpolation.cs`, `Triangulation.Refine.cs`, `Triangulation.Surfaces.cs`). For the package validation and the API tracking this is an addition only (ADR 0028).
- **API:**

  ```csharp
  double InterpolateNaturalNeighbour(Coordinate point)
  Triangulation Refine(double minAngle = 20, double? maxArea = null, int maxSteinerPoints = 100_000)
  double MinAngle(int triangle)
  double MinAngle()
  (double Above, double Below) Volume(Triangulation other)
  IReadOnlyList<IPolygon2D> SlopeZones(IReadOnlyList<double> limits, IGeometryFactory? factory = null)
  bool LineOfSight(Coordinate from, Coordinate to, double observerHeight = 0, double targetHeight = 0)
  ```

- **`InterpolateNaturalNeighbour` is Sibson interpolation.** The height at a point is the mean of the heights of the vertices it would be connected to if it were inserted, weighted by the area of their Voronoi cells that the new point would take over. It is continuous, smooth except at the vertices, **reproduces a plane exactly**, and **stays within the range of the heights** around the point (a polynomial can overshoot, the linear interpolation has creases on every edge). How it is computed, with the structure that is already there:
  - The triangles whose circumcircle contains the point (exact `InCircle`, ADR 0005) are the cavity that the point would cut out; they are found from the triangle that contains it by their neighbours.
  - The boundary of the cavity is the ring of the neighbours. The new Voronoi corners are the circumcentres of the point with every boundary edge, the old ones those of the cavity triangles, both from the circumcentre of the Voronoi diagram (ADR 0036), which is exact for slivers. The part that the new cell takes from a neighbour is the polygon of two new corners and the old corners between them; its area is the weight.
  - **The Delaunay property is required.** A triangulation with constraints (a break line, a polygon) gives the linear `InterpolateZ`, which is the right answer where a surface must have a crease. The same holds for a point on the hull (its cell is unbounded), and as a safeguard when an area cannot be computed (a circumcentre beyond double precision). A point on a vertex gives its height, a point outside the triangles and a neighbour without Z give `NaN`, as `InterpolateZ` does.
  - Tests: the definition itself as the oracle (the areas that every cell loses when the point is added, computed by the independent half-plane clipping of the Voronoi tests), a plane, the range of the heights, and the stress tests for dirty sites.
- **`Refine` is Delaunay refinement after Ruppert.** It adds points until no triangle has an angle below `minAngle` (degrees) or an area above `maxArea`. The surface stays what it was:
  - The **border and the constraints stay where they are**; an edge that is in the way of an added point (the point lies in its diametral circle) is split in the middle, so constraints become chains of shorter constrained edges (`IsConstrained` stays true on them, a border edge of a polygon stays a constraint). A triangulation from `FromPolygon` stays inside its rings, with its holes.
  - The **added points get their height from the triangulation that is refined**, linear on a split edge, interpolated in the triangle for a circumcentre, so they lie on the original surface.
  - A bad triangle is repaired by adding its circumcentre, unless that would encroach an edge, which is then split instead. The edges come first. Several points are added per round (triangles that touch are not repaired in the same round), and the triangulation is rebuilt per round with the constrained Delaunay machinery of ADR 0038, which is simpler than updating a mesh; the work is O(n log n) per round, and the rounds are few for the usual quality demands (at most 400, then it stops).
  - **Termination.** Ruppert's method is proven to stop for `minAngle` up to about 20.7 degrees when no two constraints meet at a smaller angle; in practice it stops to about 30. Above 34 degrees the call is rejected. Where the input has a smaller angle (the tip of a thin wedge) the sharp triangles at the tip cannot be avoided, and a triangle whose circumcircle is smaller than 1e-7 of the extent is left as it is. `maxSteinerPoints` ends the work in any case, and the result is then a valid triangulation, only not as fine as asked. A call that has nothing to do returns the same instance.
  - `MinAngle` is the smallest angle in degrees of a triangle (60 for an equilateral one) and of the whole triangulation (`NaN` without triangles).
  - Tests: no angle below the limit, the area, every original vertex kept, every new vertex on the surface (a plane as the oracle), the largest area, a break line that stays a chain of constrained edges on the line, a polygon with its border, the limit of the points, and stress tests on dirty sites.
- **`Volume(other)` is the cut and fill between two surfaces**, exact over the area that both cover: the two triangulations are laid over each other, every triangle of one is cut with the triangles of the other (convex clipping against three half-planes, the candidates from the index), and over every piece the difference of two planes is a plane, so the volume above and below is the same integral as `Volume(z)` (ADR 0039). `Above` is where this surface lies above the other, `Below` where it lies below; swapping the two swaps them, and `Above − Below` is the difference of the net volumes. Triangles with a vertex without Z are not counted; no common area gives (0, 0). Tests: slabs, a ramp against a plane, partial overlap, the equality with `Volume(z)` for a flat second surface, and the symmetry.
- **`SlopeZones` is the map of the slopes.** The triangles are sorted into classes by `Slope` (class 0 below the first limit, class `i` from limit `i − 1` up to limit `i`, the last from the last limit up) and every class is united (`UnionAll`) to one polygon; a class without area is an empty polygon. The limits must be finite and strictly ascending. Tests: a flat part against a ramp, the classes tile the surface, and every triangle lies in its class.
- **`LineOfSight` asks whether the ground blocks the view.** The line from the eye (the surface at `from` plus `observerHeight`) to the target (the surface at `to` plus `targetHeight`) is compared with the surface at every place where it crosses an edge or a vertex, which are the same cuts as `Drape` (ADR 0039), and the surface is linear between them, so that these places are all that has to be looked at. A line that only touches the ground (a flat field with both heights 0, a ridge exactly in the way) is free, with a tolerance of 1e-9 of the height. A point outside the surface or without a height gives `false`; where the line leaves the surface (a notch of a polygon) there is no ground to block it. Tests: a hill, flat ground, symmetry and that a higher eye or target never sees less.
- **Exceptions (ADR 0012):** `ArgumentOutOfRangeException` for coordinates or heights that are not finite, for `minAngle` outside 0 to 34, for a `maxArea` that is not greater than 0, for a negative `maxSteinerPoints` and for a triangle index out of range; `ArgumentNullException` for `null`; `ArgumentException` for limits that are not finite or not ascending; `InvalidOperationException` without a factory.
- **Playground and documentation:** entries `naturalNeighbour`, `refine`, `slopeZones`, `lineOfSight` and `surfaceVolume` with examples (the terrain examples are shared, a new one for the view over a hill and one for an excavation), sections in the concept page "Triangulation, Voronoi and terrain", glossary entries.
- **Not now:** a grid or raster of interpolated values (call `InterpolateNaturalNeighbour` per cell), natural neighbour with the break lines respected, Laplace or other interpolations, a viewshed (the area that can be seen from a point), and the conforming and the minimum-size meshes of Shewchuk (the offcentre and the concentric shells at small input angles).

## Considered Options

- Natural neighbour by inserting the point into a copy of the mesh and reading the areas from it (rejected: it copies the triangulation for every query; the cavity of the point and the areas of the Voronoi cells give the same weights from the unchanged structure)
- Laplace (non-Sibson) interpolation, which needs only edge lengths (rejected: it is not the usual reference, and its weights differ from the Sibson ones, so a user who compares with other software gets different numbers; it can be added)
- Natural neighbour with the constraints respected (rejected: the cavity logic needs the Delaunay property, and a constraint means that the surface has a crease, which the smooth interpolation is meant to remove; the fallback to the linear height is the honest answer)
- Refining by updating the mesh in place with insertions and flips (rejected for now: the rebuild per round reuses the tested constrained Delaunay code and the exact predicates, and the cost is acceptable; an incremental structure would be faster for millions of triangles)
- Refinement with the proofs of Shewchuk (offcentres, concentric shells for small input angles) (rejected for now: the size floor and the cap on the points make the method stop, and the guarantee of the angle holds where the input allows it)
- A function that returns a raster or grid of heights (rejected: a grid is a different structure of the library, and the call per cell is simple; the natural neighbour has no creases, so a coarse grid is enough)
- `Volume(other)` by sampling both surfaces on a grid (rejected: not exact; the cut of two linear surfaces is exact and fast)
- A viewshed instead of a single line of sight (rejected for now: a viewshed is a polygon from a sweep around the observer, a bigger piece of work; the line of sight is its building block and answers the question for one point)
